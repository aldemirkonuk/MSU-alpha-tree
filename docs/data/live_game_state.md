# Live game state: what we know at every instant, how late, and what it becomes when a sensor drops

Date: 2026-11-11 (last journal entry 2026-11-20). Version 0.1.0. Status: proposal under the build freeze (baseline 4 of 8). Every limit is an engineering proposal; each one says whether it is measured (and where) or assumed (and why).

This page is generated from `config/live_state/*.yaml`, `schema/live_state_changes.yaml` and `results/live_state_test/*.json` by `tools/specgen/live_state/gen_doc.py` (prose from `tools/specgen/live_state/doc_template.md`). Do not edit it by hand.

## What this page is

The live game state: every value the system holds at every instant of a game. For each value this pack fixes its source, when it updates, how late it is known, and what it becomes when a sensor disappears (the dropout rule). It deepens the one-page proposal of 19 Oct 2026 (docs/data/live_game_state.md, now generated from this pack) and sits on the K09 stored truth (config/state_machine.yaml - state_transitions, state_segments, clock_map).

The ask: Board 2026-10-20: baseline 4 = live game state deep spec (per field - source, update timing, latency, dropout rule).

The page of 19 Oct listed the fields in 10 groups. This page keeps those 10 groups and adds, for every field: where the value comes from, when it changes, how late we know it, and what it becomes when the sensor that gives it goes away. The first screen is the summary. Everything after it is reference.

## One-screen summary

**The rule.** A value is never shown or used as better than it is. Every value carries a grade, an age and the time it became known. When its sensors go away the grade moves down a fixed ladder at measured limits; it never moves up without a new observation. A value that cannot be known is "unknown" (or "off" / "none"), never empty and never a guess.

**The grade ladder.** Every value carries one grade at every instant. Best first: `confirmed` > `observed` > `inferred` > `carried` > `imputed` > `unknown`; aside: `quarantined`. A value only moves down the ladder with time; it moves up only when a sensor sees it again.

## How to read this page

- **Field tables** (the 10 groups) have eight columns.
  - *Field*: the id, the key used in the YAML, and the plain name.
  - *Values / unit*: what the field can hold, its type and unit, and the word stored when it is not known. No field is ever empty.
  - *Stored*: `key` = part of the row key; `segment` = a dimension of `state_segments` (changed only by typed transitions); `clock_map` = the clock map; `obs` = a per-frame observation table; `derived` = computed, not stored as truth; `fixed` = set before tip.
  - *Sources by authority*: the sensors, by role. `value` gives the value (the scorebook first), `confirm` agrees or disagrees with it, `timing` gives the instant, `fallback` is used when the others are away. A derived field lists its inputs.
  - *Changes on*: the transition types of `config/state_machine.yaml` (T00 to T26) that can change it, or `continuous`, `per_frame`, `on_input_change`. "Instant" is the exact definition of the moment the fact becomes true in the game.
  - *Live latency budget*: median / p95 of known time minus event time, in seconds of video time. Provisional = first held at any grade; confirmed = agreed by a second sensor. The tag in brackets says whether the budget is measured or assumed. Latency is reported, not a gate (K23).
  - *When a sensor drops*: one line per condition: the grades the value passes (with the limit of each step), the rule applied, the rule applied when the sensor is back, and the evidence tag. "Rules of profile" means the field uses the rules of its profile unchanged (section Rules and profiles).
  - *Conflict rule*: when two sources disagree, who wins the value, who wins the instant, and where the disagreement is logged.
- **A grade path** such as `observed > inferred > carried > unknown` with limits 3 s and 30 s reads: observed until the sensor goes; inferred for up to 3 s; carried up to 30 s; unknown after that.
- **Parameters** are written as `name` with their value. Every one is listed once in the table of section "What is measured and what is assumed", with its evidence.
- **Requirement ids** (R-SR, R-IV, R-SB, R-MB, R-SY, R-MD ...) are those of the live requirements. This pack answers: R-SR-01..20, R-IV-01..49, R-SB-01..05, R-MB-01..08, R-SY-01, R-SY-11, R-SY-14, R-SY-15, R-SY-19, R-SY-20, R-MD-01, R-MD-21, R-MD-22, R-MD-24, R-MK-03, R-MK-07, R-MM-03, R-AU-05, R-PR-11, R-PR-15. Ids are those of the live requirements, rev 563 of 11 Nov 2026. R-MD-21 was N-MD-01 and R-MD-22 was N-MD-02 (older repo files still use the N- names).
- Each record in the YAML also lists the table columns the field lands in (`lands_in`; a column that does not exist yet is in `schema/live_state_changes.yaml`), its invariants and its consumers, and has notes that say what happens on a tight shot, in a replay, in a break and with play-by-play only. Those are not repeated here.

**Three times per value.**

| Time | Meaning |
|---|---|
| event | The instant the fact became true in the game - a video frame (and its game clock). Example - the frame the ball is fully through the net. |
| observed | The instant a sensor showed it. Example - the frame the scorebug digit starts to change. |
| known | The instant the fused state first held the value at a given grade (known_at). Latency = known_time - event_time. |

Stored as: R-SY-11: every transition stores its effective frame (event), the observed frame per source and known_at_utc. R-MD-24: every row that can be revised carries known_at_utc.

**Two views.**

| View | What it holds and who may read it |
|---|---|
| `as_of_event` | What was true at t. For describing games, for training labels and for the anchored pass. May use values that became known later. |
| `as_of_known` | What the system knew at t. The only view a live panel, a live model input, a backtest of a live decision or a market comparison may read (no leakage, principle 6 of the state record). |

**UTC.**

| Name | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `est_utc` | Estimated real-world UTC of an event (clock_map.utc_at_from with utc_err_s). R-MK-03: a market price is joined to game state only when the UTC error is at most 30 s. |
| `known_at_utc` | est_utc of the event + stream_delay_s of the video source + processing latency. A price at time u may be compared with a model value only if that value's known_at_utc &lt;= u ("could we have acted", R-MK-07); est_utc is used for "what was true". |
| `stream_delay_s` | Delay of the recorded or streamed video against real time, per video source. Not measured yet (open question OQ-LS-2). |

**Modes.** The spec is written for `live`; the other modes differ as the table says.

| Mode | What differs |
|---|---|
| `live` | Causal. Video and feeds arrive in time order; only as_of_known exists while the game runs. This pack is written for this mode. |
| `post_blind` | Whole video, no play-by-play. Same rules; a value may be revised with later video (as_of_event); the as_of_known view is still rebuilt for backtests. |
| `post_anchored` | Whole video plus the final play-by-play. The scorebook wins values; CV and audio win instants. |
| `pbp_only` | No video. Only what the play-by-play fold can write (coarse flow states, counts, score); every CV-only field is unknown. |

- **Storage.** Stored state is home / away (R-MD-22, was N-MD-02). MSU-relative names (score_msu, margin, MSU ball) are views.
- **No empty values.** A field that cannot exist holds "off" or "none"; a field that exists but cannot be known holds "unknown". No field is ever null.
- **Latency.** K23 - latency is measured and reported per field and per game, not a gate. The budgets below exist so measured numbers have something to be compared with.
- **Replays.** A replay never changes game state (state record principle 7).
- **Units.** seconds (s), milliseconds (ms), metres (m), probability 0-1. No feet.

**Evidence tags.** Every evidence string starts with one of these tags and a colon. The lint rejects any other start.

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `measured` | on our own data; names the result file |
| `assumed` | not measured; says why this value and which measurement will replace it |
| `rule` | a rule of the 2026-27 rules book (docs/rules/PRMBB_RulesBook.pdf), with its number |
| `decision` | a recorded decision (K01-K29, D01-D45, an owner answer) |
| `requirement` | a live-requirement id or a state-record principle |
| `config` | mirrors a value of another config file of the repo |
| `definition` | true by construction (a rule of this pack, an identity) |
| `published` | an outside source with a citation (none yet - the web research step was skipped for 0.1.0) |
| `vendor claim` | a number a vendor states about its own product (none yet) |

**How a dropout rule is read.** How a dropout rule is read. grade_path lists the grades a value passes while the condition of `when` lasts. A path that starts with confirmed, observed names the two grades the value may hold when the condition starts (one start, not a step). Each later grade is one step down. step_at gives the age (seconds since the condition started) of each step - 0, the name of a limit, or on_return (the step is taken when the condition ends). Without step_at the default applies - the limits whose unit is seconds, in the order written, are the ages of the LAST steps; a step with no limit happens when the condition starts. A rule with steps and more time limits than steps must write step_at (lint); a rule with no step reads its limits as thresholds. Limits that are not times (a count, a share, a number of readings) are thresholds the rule reads, never step ages. The reference code (fuse.py, class GradeTrack) reads every rule this way.

## Grades

| Rank | Grade | Meaning | May feed | Written to dq today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `confirmed` | Observed, and agreed by a second independent sensor inside the confirm window. | live_panel, live_models, stats, training_labels | `observed` |
| 2 | `observed` | Read from a sensor that can see it, inside that sensor's freshness window. | live_panel, live_models, stats, training_labels | `observed` |
| 3 | `inferred` | Derived by a named rule before any sensor shows it (a made basket seen by CV adds the points), or dead-reckoned inside the measured safe horizon. An inferred value is a claim - the rule states how often it must be right (promise). | live_panel, live_models | `inferred` |
| 4 | `carried` | The last value, held while its sensors are away, with its age and (where the profile gives one) a guaranteed interval. Not a claim that nothing changed. | live_panel_marked_stale, live_models_with_interval | `carried` |
| 5 | `imputed` | A model estimate beyond the carry limit (off-screen positions). | live_panel_marked_estimate | `imputed` |
| 6 | `unknown` | Nothing reliable. The explicit value "unknown" is stored. | nothing | `imputed` |
| 9 | `quarantined` | Contradicted by another source or by an invariant; held out of every consumer until reviewed. | nothing | `quarantined` |

- A grade path only moves down the ladder. Any move up needs a new observation (or a review answer for quarantined).
- Stats and training labels read only confirmed and observed values of the anchored pass.
- The existing dq enum (observed, inferred, carried, imputed, quarantined) is kept; confirmed and unknown are proposed additions (schema/live_state_changes.yaml). Until they exist, dq_enum above is the value written.
- Stamps: Every group of fields carries src (sensor), conf (0-1), grade, age_s (seconds since the last observation of that value) and known_at_utc.

## Rules and profiles

A field names one profile and overrides only what differs. A profile fixes the sources, the latency budget, the conflict rule and the dropout rules.

### Rule library

| Rule | What it does |
|---|---|
| `HOLD_UNTIL_EVENT` | A piecewise-constant value keeps its grade for as long as at least one sensor that would see it change is watching (a change detector). When no change detector is watching, CARRY applies. |
| `CARRY` | Keep the last value; grade carried; age_s counts up from the last observation. Past the field's carried limit the value becomes unknown (or stays carried with its interval where the profile says so). |
| `DEAD_RECKON` | Clock only. While the running flag is running, the estimate advances 1.0 s per second of video; while stopped it holds. Grade inferred up to the inferred limit, then carried. The estimate always carries the guaranteed interval [last reading - elapsed video time, last reading] (floored at 0). |
| `CUE_RECKON` | Clocks, their running flags and the shot-clock reset. A stop cue (whistle, horn, CV dead ball), a start cue (throw-in touch, tap) or a reset cause seen by CV while the bug is away sets the running flag (or the reset value) at the cue instant and re-anchors the reckoning there. The cue is an observation of the flag, not of the clock value: the flag is inferred from the cue and its limits count from the cue; for the clock VALUE only the carried limit counts from the cue, the inferred limit still counts from the last READING, and the grade never rises (a value already carried stays carried). The estimate itself gets better with cues; the claim does not get longer (replay harness, journal of 20 Nov). |
| `FOLLOW_FIELD` | A value whose own sensor is away follows another field that is still observed and moves with it by rule (the shot clock runs and stops with the game clock, Rule 2-11). It is graded inferred while the rule gives a limit, then carried. The followed field must itself be observed. |
| `RULE_DEFAULT` | A value that no source states takes the default a rule or a requirement gives, at grade inferred, and is flagged. It stays inferred until a source states it (LATE_CORRECTION if the source differs). |
| `SNAP_AND_LOG` | When the sensor returns, the value snaps to the new reading after scorebug_up_after_reads (2 readings) agreeing readings. The first reading after a gap is held at grade inferred; the agreeing reading that follows makes it observed. If the reading differs from the held estimate by more than the field's tolerance, the miss is logged (a latency_report row always; a state_corrections row when a stored transition is replaced) and the held stretch is re-graded in the as_of_event view only. |
| `CONFIRM_WINDOW` | A provisional value from the fast sensor is confirmed when the slower independent sensor shows the same change inside the window. If the window closes without it, a flag is raised (scoreboard_flags) and the value stays provisional (inferred), never silently confirmed. |
| `FREEZE_ON_REPLAY` | While replay_flag is on, no game-state field changes; observations are stored raw and marked replay. Transitions are held back until the replay detector has had replay_guard_s (1 s) to object. |
| `FREEZE_ON_BREAK` | During a break (ad, studio, full-screen graphic) every game-state field is carried at its last value; on return REACQUIRE applies. |
| `REACQUIRE` | After a break or a cut back to the main camera - period, clocks, score and counts are re-read from the bug (SNAP_AND_LOG); lineups are unverified until play-by-play substitution rows or identity locks confirm them; possession and ball state are unknown until the first observed control. |
| `MAX_DWELL` | A carried status value may not outlive the longest time the rules or physics allow for it (max_dwell table). Past that time the old value becomes unknown; where the rules give what follows, the rule names it as next_value, at grade inferred. |
| `AUTHORITY` | When sources disagree on a VALUE - official scorebook (pbp_final, then pbp_feed) > arena scoreboard > broadcast scorebug > CV inference (Rule 2-9.13). On an INSTANT - cv_events and audio > scorebug change time minus its measured lag > play-by-play clock second. Raw readings are never overwritten. |

## Degradation levels

What the broadcast shows decides what can be known. The level is itself a field (`degradation_level`). The columns are the profiles.

| Level | When | Clocks | Counters | Status | Lineups | Positions | Derived | Live panel may show |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `D0_full` | main wide camera, bug readable, feed healthy | observed | confirmed | observed | confirmed | observed | observed | everything |
| `D1_bug_away` | main wide camera, bug hidden or unreadable | inferred up to clock_inferred_max_s (3 s), then carried, then unknown | observed while CV watches the ball and rim, else carried | observed | observed | observed | worst input | clock shown with its age; score marked provisional |
| `D2_off_main` | tight shot, bench, crowd, reverse angle (play may be live) | as read if the bug is visible, else D1 | as read if the bug is visible, else carried | carried up to its max dwell, then unknown | observed in live play, unverified after an unseen dead ball | imputed, then unknown | worst input | positions hidden; counts as read |
| `D3_replay` | replay or slow motion | carried | carried | carried | carried | unknown | carried | frozen state, marked replay |
| `D4_break` | ad break, studio, full-screen graphic, frozen picture or repeated frames | carried | carried | carried, then unknown | carried, unverified on return | unknown | carried | frozen state, marked break |
| `D5_no_video` | stream lost | unknown after clock_carried_max_s (30 s) | carried; from the feed if healthy | unknown | from the feed if healthy | unknown | worst input | feed-only view |

**`feed_stale`** (a flag on top of any level - no feed row for feed_stale_after_s (90 s)): scorebook-only values (who, what, foul type, substitutions) stop updating; confirmed values that needed the feed stay observed.

When the shot type is unsure the stricter level applies (meta profile). Levels D0 to D4 change only through T25 (what the broadcast shows). D5_no_video and the feed_stale flag are set by the heartbeats of the stream and of the feed, outside the typed transitions: T25 of config/state_machine.yaml lists camera cuts, replays, graphics and the bug only. A worse level applies at once; a better one waits for the return rule of the field (SNAP_AND_LOG, REACQUIRE). The columns are the profiles: clocks = clock, counters = counter, and so on. Fixed fields are not degraded at any level. Meta fields are what sets the level; they follow the meta profile.

## Reconciliation windows

The scorebook wins values. CV and audio win instants. A raw reading is never overwritten.

**Who wins a value:** `pbp_final` > `pbp_feed` > `arena_scoreboard` > `scorebug_ocr` > `cv_events`.

**Who wins an instant:** `cv_events` > `audio` > `scorebug_ocr` > `pbp_final`.

The arena scoreboard is in the order because Rule 2-9.13 names it; it is not a sensor of this pack (it is seen only when the camera shows it).

**Confirm windows.**

| Window | Parameter | Value | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| make to bug | `make_confirm_window_s` | 4 s | a CV made basket must show on the bug inside this window (K02 start value; then per field p99 + window_margin_s, kept inside make_window_min_s and make_window_max_s) | decision: K02 and R-SB-03; measured largest lag 2.60 s of 10 (results/w3_intake/made_basket_lag.md) |
| pbp second to bug | `pbp_to_bug_window_s` | 12 s | a play-by-play made field goal must show on the bug inside this window, referenced to the play-by-play clock second (p99 11 s + window_margin_s). It is a different parameter from make_confirm_window_s because the scorer's clock second is itself off | measured: measured_sensors.json score.made_fg_lag_vs_pbp_clock_s (n = 1,344; 98.7 % within 10 s) |
| foul to bug count | `foul_to_bug_window_s` | 42 s | the bug team-foul count must follow a play-by-play foul inside this window (p99 41 s + window_margin_s) | measured: measured_sensors.json bug_fields.team_foul_lag_vs_pbp_clock_s (n = 566) |

Re-set rule: K02 and R-SB-03 - after each game a window is re-set to the measured 99th percentile of its lag plus window_margin_s (1 s). The make window stays inside make_window_min_s (3 s) and make_window_max_s (15 s).

**Tolerances.** Counters 0 (any difference is a flag). Clocks clock_tolerance_s (R-SB-03 tightened: scorebug against play-by-play within +-1 s of game clock).

**Where a disagreement is logged.**

| Log | What goes there |
|---|---|
| `scoreboard_flags` | a confirm window that closed without its confirmation, or a bug value that disagrees with the scorebook |
| `state_corrections` | a stored value replaced after review or by an official correction (the raw reading stays) |
| `cv_events_unmatched` | a CV event with no play-by-play row (never discarded, principle 4; R-SR-14) |
| `identity_review` | a lineup from the scorebook that the identity locks contradict (review item of the identity pack) |
| `registry_change_log` | a registry value changed after it was first set |
| `latency_report` | per game and field - event, observed and known times, per grade (K23) |

**Late and corrected rows.** LATE_CORRECTION - new state_version; old versions stay readable; as_of_known is immutable for instants already past. A late or corrected source row writes new transitions with supersedes_id and a new state_version; the old version stays readable, and as_of_known never changes for instants already past.

**How a latency budget is put together.** A timing block of a profile or a field is known_time - event_time in video time: the delay of the sensor named in "by" plus frame_to_state_s. A derived field is its slowest input plus derived_compute_s; when some inputs are much slower than the ones that move the value, the field lists the inputs its timing is written for under timing.driven_by. capture_to_frame_s and state_to_view_s sit outside the block: together with stream_delay_s they turn video time into known_at_utc and into what a viewer sees.
